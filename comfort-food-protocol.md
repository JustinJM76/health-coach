# Comfort-food / binge protocol

_Designed 2026-04-29 (Day 9). Living document — refine after each time it fires._

## Purpose

Justin has a wired connection between **physical/emotional discomfort** and craving **rich indulgent food** (pizza, fried, meatball grinder, mac & cheese, ice cream — comfort generally, not any specific food). On a good day, willpower handles it. On a really bad day, willpower doesn't.

This protocol is what runs on the bad day, so we don't have to rely on willpower.

## When this fires

Trigger conditions Justin has identified:
- **Exhaustion** (acute, e.g., post-dentist; chronic, e.g., bad-sleep weeks)
- **Financial stress** (historical trigger, may recur)
- **Physical/emotional discomfort generally** (illness, injury, painful event)

NOT a primary trigger for Justin: routine work busyness. He gets busy without getting stressed.

Sign that this is firing: a *sudden, vivid* craving for rich/indulgent food, often hot, often easy to order/grab. Different from baseline hunger — feels like seeking, not feeding.

## The three-step cascade

### Step 1: Name it (5 seconds)

Out loud or in your head:

> "I'm having a comfort-food urge right now. The trigger is [exhaustion / pain / stress / I don't know yet]."

Naming the urge breaks autopilot. The brain can't keep running an unconscious script after you've made it conscious. This step alone disarms about a third of the urge before you do anything else.

### Step 2: Primer + 20 minutes (Justin's own design)

Eat a primer from your normal rotation — **cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, hard-boiled egg, deli turkey roll-ups**, whatever's available. Then **wait 20 minutes** before making any further food decision.

Why this works:
- Protein blunts the reward-seeking chemistry directly (GLP-1 + CCK satiety hormones)
- The 20-minute window lets the craving peak and start to decline (urges are time-bounded; they don't sustain)
- Drinking 12 oz of water alongside the primer is a free add — dehydration often masquerades as craving

After the 20 minutes, re-check honestly:
- "Do I still want pizza, or am I just hungry?"
- If just hungry → eat your normal lunch/dinner from the template
- If still want pizza → step 3

### Step 3: If the craving still wins — controlled indulgence

If after primer + 20 min you still want the comfort food, **you are allowed to have it.** No moralizing. The protocol's job is to prevent a bad day from becoming a bad week, not to enforce purity.

But with limits:

- **One serving.** Not "extra slice for later." Not "the whole grinder." Order a single serving size. If it comes in a multi-pack (pizza), give the rest to family or trash it before you start eating.
- **Sitting down, not standing at the counter.** Counter-eating bypasses satiety registration.
- **Pair with a vegetable.** Side salad, raw veg, leftovers — anything green. Doesn't have to be much. The point is "this is a meal, not a hit."
- **No alcohol-on-top.** Don't compound. Skip the beer with the pizza this once.
- **Eat slowly.** The food is already there; you've already "won" the urge. No need to rush.
- **After eating: 10-minute walk.** Outside if possible.

### Damage control (the next morning)

If you go through step 3, the next morning:
- **Weigh in normally.** Don't skip the scale. The number will be up — that's water/sodium, not fat. Read it correctly.
- **Run the program normally.** Don't skip the workout. Don't skip the primer. Do not "make up for it" with extra restriction — that's the spiral.
- **One slip ≠ ruined week.** Two slips in a row is a pattern; one is data.
- Tell the coach what happened. Not for absolution — for pattern-spotting.

## Specific exhaustion variant (today's case)

When the trigger is exhaustion specifically, the secondary moves before step 2:
- **12 oz of water first** (fatigue + dehydration often = phantom hunger)
- **5 min outside in sunlight** (cortisol/circadian re-anchor)
- **Caffeine if it's before 2 PM and you haven't already had your second cup**
- Then run step 2 if the urge persists

## Specific financial-stress variant

When the trigger is financial stress (chronic baseline pressure):
- Step 2 still works, but the trigger isn't going away after 20 min the way exhaustion does
- Pre-game the kitchen: when finances are tight, make sure the primer slot is well-stocked (cottage cheese, yogurt, eggs). Don't keep impulsive comfort food in the house "just in case."
- Long game: the protocol won't fix financial stress. Other tools for that (which are out of scope here, but worth naming: budget review, conversation with the relevant party, walk before opening the laptop).

## What this is NOT

- A way to "earn" a pizza by suffering through cottage cheese first. The primer isn't punishment; it's diagnostic — "is this hunger or seeking?"
- A rule you have to follow perfectly. If you skip step 2 once, the protocol still works the next time. There's no debt.
- A weight-loss accelerator. It's a slip-prevention floor.
- A binge-eating treatment. If you ever feel a true binge urge (alone, secretive, "I have to eat this whole thing right now and nobody knows"), text the coach. That's a different protocol and we'd design it together.

## Coach contact

Optional, never required. You can text the coach at any step. The protocol is yours to run; coach is a sounding board, not a gatekeeper.

## Logging

When this protocol fires (or almost fires, like 2026-04-29), note in that day's journal:
- What the trigger was
- Which step caught it (1, 2, 3, or "I bypassed it")

- How you felt 1 hour later

This is how the protocol gets better over time.
