The week-2 stretch where the program transitioned from "running new experiments" to "running the system on autopilot." Three structural events:
1. Comfort-food protocol drafted from Justin's own data (Day 9, post-dentist)
2. Recipe rotation expanded to four entries; "Friday flex" pattern named
3. Hard-day adherence proven on a 4,500-lb-of-concrete day (Day 12)

## Major events

### Day 7 (Sun 4/27): smart self-titration on intervals
Monday workout: prescribed 8 elliptical sprints over 20 min; Justin ran 6 with longer rest. Did not grind broken reps. Coach affirmed: *"You self-titrated to your actual capacity instead of either grinding 8 broken reps or quitting. That's the difference between training and just suffering."* Pattern continues throughout the week.

### Day 8 (Mon 4/28): lat pulldown form puzzle, Justin debugs via YouTube
Setup felt off; Justin's own diagnosis was "door too short or bands too long." Coach proposed (incorrectly) it was a height-geometry issue requiring kneeling. Justin investigated independently via YouTube, found the actual fix: stance position + pull angle. Coach owned the bad diagnosis. Filed: *Justin's investigative response (debug instead of grind) is itself a sustainability signal.*

### Day 9 (Tue 4/29): MAJOR — comfort-food urge identified and resisted
After dental procedure, Justin felt an acute craving for "comfort food" (pizza, mac & cheese, fried). Resisted, stuck to plan. Volunteered the disclosure plus stated readiness to design a protocol:

> "I am certain that 2 weeks ago I would have given in to it. Still, I think I need to continue reprogramming myself so that if I have a *really* bad day I don't have a major slip that I'll regret."

**`comfort-food-protocol.md` drafted from Justin's own articulated structure.** Three-step cascade:
1. Name the urge (5 sec; disrupts autopilot)
2. Primer + 20-min wait (Justin's own backup plan)
3. Controlled indulgence with damage-control (if step 2 doesn't catch it)

Plus exhaustion-specific and financial-stress-specific variants. Triggers identified: exhaustion, financial stress, physical/emotional discomfort. NOT triggered by routine work busyness.

Also: 7-day trailing average first calculated on Day 9 (244.5 lb).

### Day 10 (Wed 4/30): Greek lemon chicken — first cook + chickpea wife loop closed
Justin cooked the new Thursday "real dinner" recipe. Family hit; one daughter grumbled pre-dinner but cleared her plate. **Wife "really appreciated" the chickpeas added to the salad** (her plant-protein request from Day 5 showed up on a plate Day 10). First household-stakeholder feedback loop closed cleanly. Recipe locked in for every-other-week rotation.

### Day 11 (Thu 5/1): Italian beef meatballs from scratch
Justin opted to make meatballs from scratch rather than frozen. Recipe drafted with panade technique, baked-not-fried for volume. Family approved. v1 iteration notes: underseasoned (will add Italian seasoning + onion + extra garlic for v2). 16 meatballs at ~1.5 oz vs planned 20 at ~1.25 oz; portion math adjusted.

**Friday flex pattern named:** when family wants a dish that's not on-program, cook it for them, eat a parallel option for self. Avoids martyr-meal AND kitchen sterilization.

### Day 12 (Fri 5/2): patio build day — hard-day adherence proven
Justin helped a friend build a patio: **75 × 60 lb = 4,500 lb of concrete moved over 11 hours of physical labor**. Coach pre-strategized the day Friday night; Justin executed cleanly:
- Cottage cheese primer in the cooler
- Single turkey sandwich for lunch
- 3 slices pizza at 9pm dinner
- No snacking through 11 hours
- Hydrated steadily

Likely net deficit despite the pizza. **Hard-day adherence #4** (after yard-work day, post-dentist comfort-food urge, late-night dance comp). Pattern: the system runs even when conditions are bad.

### Day 13 (Sun 5/3): meal planning + recovery
Recovery day after the patio. Coach pushed for lighter Monday workout. Week's dinner plan locked: chili leftovers, pulled chicken Wed, salmon Thu, etc. **Meal-planning principle filed:** *avoid same-protein same-pattern on consecutive days, even if both are "good."*
