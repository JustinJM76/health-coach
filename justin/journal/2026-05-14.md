## The mattress finding

Larissa had been complaining Justin's side of the mattress sagged and was pulling her toward him. They rotated the mattress 180° as an experiment. Result:
- **Justin:** best sleep he can remember, no morning back pain, far fewer position changes, woke naturally feeling great.
- **Larissa:** slept horribly, tossed and turned, woke sore (she inherited the worn spot).

Clean variable isolation — one change, opposite outcomes for the two sleepers. **Conclusion: the mattress is worn out.** Going mattress shopping this weekend.

**Why this matters for the program:**
- Two mechanisms were likely both running — (1) sleep fragmentation from constant repositioning (micro-arousals cost deep sleep; "changed position far less" → consolidated sleep, which is why 6.5 hrs beat his usual ~8), and (2) poor spinal support driving the morning low-back pain, which is mechanically plausible given Justin's disc-injury history.
- The original bedtime-anchor experiment was **behavioral** (remember to go to bed earlier). A new mattress is **structural** — works without requiring memory or willpower. Structural > behavioral for sustainability.
- If the new mattress delivers what last night suggests, afternoon snack intensity and stress reactivity both ease for free — chronic sleep debt drives both.

**Coach guidance for the shopping trip:** medium-firm (evidence zone for back health; too soft = the sag they just diagnosed); test lying in actual sleep position 10+ min, not a hand-press; both Justin and Larissa test every candidate since they now have opposite data points; consider split-firmness models; treat it as a buy-the-good-one purchase.
