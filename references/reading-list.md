## Notes on critical reading

- **Treat nutrition science as probabilistic, not deterministic.** Most claims have caveats. "X causes Y" is almost always "X is associated with Y in [these populations] [in this dosage] [in this context]."
- **Beware of n=1 enthusiasm.** Influencers and authors who built a brand on one diet/protocol will overstate that protocol's universality. The fact that something worked for them is weak evidence.
- **The honest-broker test:** does the source acknowledge counter-evidence? Sources that present everything as settled are less trustworthy than sources that wrestle with disagreements in the literature.
- **Mechanism vs. outcome:** "this affects pathway X" (mechanism) is a much weaker claim than "people who do this lose more weight in randomized trials" (outcome). Programs often confuse the two.
